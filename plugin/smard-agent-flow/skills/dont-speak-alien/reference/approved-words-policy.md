# Approved-words policy (concept note)
Concept source: ASD-STE100 Issue 9, general introduction, Part 1 Sections 1 and 9. Paraphrased.
- STE pairs its writing rules with a controlled dictionary of common technical-writing words.
- Each approved word has one meaning and one part of speech, so readers cannot misread it.
- Where English has synonyms, the dictionary approves one and rejects the others.
- A rejected word lists approved alternatives; the writer swaps the word or rebuilds the sentence.
- Words outside the dictionary are allowed only as technical nouns (parts, materials, units, software terms) or technical verbs (specialized actions).
- Technical nouns and verbs must be short, widely understood, and used the same way every time.
- Never use a technical noun as a verb, or a technical verb as a noun.
- Spelling follows American English.
- NOTE: this skill does NOT ship the STE dictionary. It is a copyrighted ASD work.
- Substitute rule: prefer the simplest common English word; use one meaning per word consistently.
- Keep technical names, product names, code, and quoted text unchanged.
